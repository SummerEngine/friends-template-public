# Backdrop (`demo/style/backdrop*.gd`, client only)

> The game's world look (a bright test facility, `style/surfaces.gd`) builds a plain tiled hall around every level and the lobby yard
> instead: `style/room_shell.gd`, called through `Backdrop.for_level` / `for_lobby`. The show mode (cast `mode: "show"`) has the party
> look instead, an open-air world in the cast's brand colours: see "The party look" at the end of this page; cast `world.style` `"night"` makes it a dark
> sci-fi world instead ("The night look", the last section). Everything between is the
> comic look's backdrop, reachable in dev runs (`--look=comic` after the `--`) and checked by `tools/backdrop_check.gd` in that look.

The world around and far below every level and the lobby yard: decor only (no collision, no shadows, no lights, no textures). Built from primitives
in code; nothing is imported.

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `backdrop.gd` | `Backdrop.for_level(number, data, origin)`, `for_lobby(origin)`, `apply_sky(environment, number)`, `level_spec(data)`, `enabled` (tools only) |
| `backdrop_themes.gd` | `PLAN` (24 rows `[kind, time]`), `TIMES` (sky, fog, colour shift), palettes, `plan(n)`, `seed_of(n, kind)`, `tone()` |
| `backdrop_scatter.gd` | the rules and the placement (pure, seeded): near world, three horizon layers, islands, cranes, billboards, clouds, blimp, birds |
| `backdrop_meshes.gd` | box, pyramid, cone, cylinder, sphere, bird (shared vertices, no normals) |
| `backdrop.gdshader`, `backdrop_outline.gdshader`, `backdrop_ground.gdshader`, `backdrop_motion.gdshaderinc` | flat 3-tone fill with windows/poster stripes, screen-space ink line (next pass), the ground map, drift + haze |

## What is drawn
* **Far below**: the world's tops end 30 m under the lowest walkable surface of the level (streets 16 m lower). Kind per level: `city` (rooftops, streets, parks),
  `yard` (container stacks, silos, mounds), `park` (fields, hedges, river, trees, tiny houses), `cloud` (a sea of clouds). The flat navy void plane is
  hidden while a backdrop is shown (`LevelWorld` hook); the kill height is unchanged.
* **Horizon**: three layers (380-620, 620-900, 900-1250 m from the course) of skyline towers, hills or cloud towers, hazier and taller with distance, with a
  bump towards the goal; tower cranes, billboards (sunburst posters, no lettering) on the first layer.
* **Islands**: grass disc on a rock cone with trees and tiny houses (towers in city, containers and a crane in yard), 9 per level (13 in cloud).
* **Motion**, all in the vertex shader (no CPU work per frame): drifting clouds, a blimp, a flock of birds flapping. They fly in lanes along the longer side of the
  course box, beside it.
* **Theme per level**: `Themes.PLAN[n - 1]` = kind and time of day (day / dusk / night); the time recolours the sky (`apply_sky`), the fog, every backdrop colour
  and lights windows. Seed = `seed_of(number, kind)`: the same level always grows the same backdrop.
* Colours are desaturated and cooler than the course and fade into the sky colour with distance; each piece has a thin ink line (about 1.6 px, takes on the haze).

## Rules (checked by `tools/backdrop_check.gd`, headless)
* Every piece that is not drifting is entirely >= 25 m below the lowest walkable surface, or >= 12 m (in practice >= 18, islands >= 40) from the bounding box of
  the course (blocks, movers with their reach, shots, path, start, goal) on x/z - so never above or beside the route.
* Drifting pieces keep >= 12 m from that box across their lane.

## Cost
About 1000-1500 instances per level (quality Low: 55 %, Medium 80 %), 5 MultiMeshes with an outline pass each plus one ground quad: +6 draw calls, about +30 %
triangles of a level (everything is drawn, the multimeshes have one big bounding box). Built the first time a level is shown (about 4 ms), then kept out of the tree.
Measured with `tools/backdrop_shots.gd --perf=N` (offscreen, M1 Max, 1280x720): level 12 play view 0.75 -> 0.77 ms per frame, level 7 0.79 -> 0.82 ms.

## Tools
* `tools/backdrop_shots.gd`: frames (gameplay camera, wide) of levels and the lobby, `--backdrop=off` for no backdrop, `--perf=<level>`.
* `tools/backdrop_check.gd`: the rules above for all levels and the lobby, instance counts, build times.

## The lobby kit (the show's yard)

The show's lobby yard (`WalkInLobby.show_yard()`: more than one circle) is NOT built from the party backdrop but from the race levels' kit, so the yard and the levels read as one place: `lobby/lobby_kit.gd` draws, in one mesh, ONE chamfered deck slab in the P tone (`BlockKit.floor_block`; the deck has the 1.54 m tile grid's size, 14 x 14 tiles' worth for the eight-round lineup, `WalkInLobby.show_tiles_x()` across, so every circle stands where the grid put it; nothing is drawn on the grid), the pillow walls on it (`BlockKit.plain_block(..., "navy", 1)`: the S tone, vinyl, a box rounded by up to half its thickness; planted 3 cm in the deck) and 8 columns of pillow segments (`PillarKit.support`, S and white, x at 0.22 and 0.65 of the deck's half width) down to the slime sea 11 m below, and around it the candy world of the daylight levels (`CandyWorld.build`, centred on the deck, the pillars' feet stand in the slime, "CandyWorld" below; the sky is the environment's: `LevelWorld.set_active_level` takes the daylight stage environment when no level is shown).
The collision follows the same size (`lobby_space.gd architecture()`: slab 21.3 x 20.9 m, walls 5.65 m; the single-circle yards keep 34.6 x 24 m). The circles' plates and signs are `lobby/pick_plate.gd` and `lobby/lobby_sign.gd` (docs/party-show.md "Pick a round"). `tools/lobby_frames.gd` renders it, `tools/mesh_lint.sh --lobby` lints it (pseudo-level 99).
The minigame yards (one circle) keep `Backdrop.for_lobby` and the party look.

## The party look (show mode: the lobby and the show levels)

The show is not a test chamber: with cast `mode` `"show"` the lobby yard and every show level are built in the **party look**
(`style/party_look.gd` is the palette and the surfaces, `style/party_backdrop.gd` the open-air world, `style/party_motion.gdshader` the
confetti and bunting, `levels/level_environment.gd` the sun and sky). `Backdrop.for_level` / `for_lobby` return `PartyBackdrop.build` instead of
`RoomShell`; `Surfaces.spec()` hands out the party surfaces, block colours, signage and light in the same shape as the facility's `LOOK`, so the
whole course (walls, track, curbs, hop blocks, movers, ice, boards) takes them without any other change. The level JSON is untouched.
Dev runs can look at the facility (`--look=facility`) or the comic look (`--look=comic`) instead, after the `--`.

**Palette**, all derived from `brand.colors` (up to 3; greys and near-blacks do not count as colours): P the first colour with colour in it
(the course: track, walls, curbs), S the next colour at least 0.06 of hue away from P, else a sky blue (a peach when P itself is blue)
(sky, accent walls), A the third colour, else a burnt shade of P (P turned 0.04 of hue towards red, saturated and dark: movers, flags; never another hue), X and Y two neighbours of P on the wheel,
clear of the other roles (hop blocks, confetti, bunting). A cast without colours gets coral, sky blue and a burnt coral. Each role has `main` (candy), `deep`,
`soft` and `pale` tones, and the rules that keep any palette legible are enforced when the palette is made: the track is a saturated
bright tone of P (luminance 0.4 and more, so the hazard red reads on it) against a paler checker (contrast 1.5), the wall and curb stripes read on
the cream (contrast 1.65 and 1.75), the hop blocks stand out of the track (1.5), the hazard red (`#f23a4e`, the same for every brand) and the dark ink (lettering on the track) read on it. `tools/party_look_check.gd`
runs these rules over 64 palettes (greys, one colour, two near hues, dark, pastel, vivid, none).
The first brand colour also leads the surfaces that are not sky: the hexagon tiles of LAST TILE STANDING are three tones of P (`tiles`: P at full strength, a deeper and a paler tone, each at least 1.15 of contrast from the
next), and the lobby's signboard banners are P itself (THE SHOW) and tones of it (races, survival rounds, the final: `signs`). A near-black brand colour is the `ink` (text, outlines) and a light grey one the `trim`
(sign frames): neither is ever a floor, a wall or a tile, so a brand of black, white and one colour gets that colour everywhere and its black and grey as ink and trim. The Summer cast (`#f0b566`, `#141414`, `#d9d9d9`)
gets an amber floor, amber hexagons and amber banners with `#141414` lettering.

| What | Look |
|---|---|
| track (`floor`) | 2 m checker of a saturated tone of P on a paler one; world-aligned, so it runs on across blocks |
| curbs (`base`, the sides of ground) | P deep and cream in 0.75 m bands |
| walls (`wall`) | 1.5 m vertical stripes of P and cream; bunting of every palette colour along the top of a tall wall, only where the line is at least 3 m over the walkable ground in front of it (`style/bunting_rule.gd`: never on stair risers, low ledges, floor-level sides or in the podium shot); accent walls (`wall_brand`) in S |
| hop blocks (`block`) | pillow-round (0.3 m), one palette colour each, rim light |
| movers, ice, steel | A (checker top, banded sides), S pale glossy, cream |
| sky and sun | S's sky tone (saturation 0.6) on top, a tint of it (0.24) at the horizon and a deeper tone (0.36) for the sea, so the white clouds, the track and the islands stand out of it; light fog (1 / 2500 per m); one warm sun (1.3) over a low ambient (0.45); ACES tone mapping at exposure 0.72, for contrast (no milky haze) |
| world | a sea of soft clouds 60 m under the lowest walkable surface, 11 candy hills and tall party hats on the horizon (250-420 m), 22 floating islands (a disc on a cone, with lollipops, party hats, gift boxes, balloons), 26 loose balloons, half of the islands beyond the goal; the course's own ground slabs stand on cones (`Supports`); confetti falls and turns all around the course |

The surface shader (`style/surface.gdshader`) has the candy patterns (`pattern` 1 checker, 2 vertical stripes, 3 bands, 4 dots, `tint2`,
`pattern_size`, filtered so they do not shimmer) and a soft rim light; a surface of `Surfaces.LOOK` and the party spec may use them.

**Rules** (checked by `tools/party_look_check.gd`, headless): every piece of the lit batches (`Spheres`, `Balls`, `Cylinders`, `Cones`, `Boxes`) is
entirely 25 m under the course's lowest walkable surface or at least 14 m from the course's box on x/z, never above or beside the route;
`Supports` hang straight under the course's own ground (never under a beam, roof or stage with another solid below it: a cone there would hang in the play space, as the one under the hammer gantry of CIRCUIT BREAKER did across the camera); decor never fills the lens: the lit decor dither-fades inside 2 m of the camera and the bunting and confetti are not drawn inside 1.6 m; `Bunting` sits on the tall walls; `Confetti` columns never start inside the course's box
below 8 m over its highest point. Nothing has collision, casts a shadow or is a light.

**Cost.** Nine draw calls (five lit MultiMeshes, supports, bunting, confetti, the sea plane), 600-1100 instances and about 34 000 triangles
at quality High (55 % of the instances at Low, Android's default); everything is drawn (one bounding box per MultiMesh). One light, the sun. Seeded:
a level always grows the same world, built in 2-3 ms the first time the level is shown. Measured with `tools/party_perf.gd` (offscreen, M1 Max,
1280x720, other agents sharing the GPU, so times are indicative): the facility room is 116 / 111 / 99 draw calls and 94 000 / 105 000 /
146 000 primitives (level 14, level 19, lobby), the party look 125 / 120 / 109 draw calls (+9) and 148 000 / 160 000 / 179 000 primitives (the
backdrop's 34 000 and the rest in the course's own meshes, not isolated); frame cost is within noise on the Mobile renderer (0.8-1.0 ms both ways) and not worse on
the Compatibility renderer (the phones': 2.6-3.5 ms against 4.3-5.1 ms in that run), which draws 252-263 against 236-247 calls on level 14.

## The night look (show mode, cast `world.style` `"night"`)

The show's second world, for a team whose logo or brand is dark, whose product is tech, games or sci-fi, or whose `team.style` reads that way.
One switch, not a second engine: `TeamCast.world_style()` (`"party"` by default, `"night"`, or `"auto"` = night when the first brand colour
is near black, HSV value under 0.30) turns on `Party.night()`, and the same palette roles, spec shape and backdrop builder come out in dark tones:
`style/party_look.gd` makes the night palette (`_night`: the same keys as the party palette), `style/night_look.gd` is its `Surfaces.LOOK` spec (pure data), `style/night_backdrop.gd`
the world (it reuses the party world's `Batch`, shapes, placement and supports), `style/night_sky.gdshader` the sky (`levels/level_environment.gd` builds it from `light`),
`style/surface.gdshader` gets thin glowing lines (patterns 5 grid, 6 lines along v, 7 lines along u, in `tint2` over a checker of `tint` and `tint3`; `glow`, `line`) and `emit` (a surface's own colour shines).
`Backdrop.for_level` / `for_lobby` pick the builder; nothing else in the game knows the difference (the HUD is the same).

**Style of the real thing** (the brand's logo and web app: the studio's design tokens and landing page): a near-black canvas (`#222` panels, `#050507` to `#101010` gradients, black behind the logo);
a faceted metal sun in silver on it; one warm amber accent (`#f0b566`, soft `#f5cf95`, muted `#6b5836`) used sparingly for light: rings, glow, call-to-action; big blurred radial glows
(amber or white, 10-20 % opacity) over the dark, a faint grid and a few tiny stars; bold wide white lettering; dense, hard-edged, tool-like, never candy.

**Palette** from the brand: P the first brand colour with colour in it (lines, trim, embers; lifted towards white until its luminance is 0.34 so a blue still shines), S the next colour of another hue, else the brand's
light grey (the silver), A the third colour, else an ember tone of P, X silver, Y a pale tint of P. A cast without colours gets a cyan accent. The void tints the sky, floors, walls and tiles: the brand's
near-black when it has one, else a very dark tone of P. `ink` stays the near-black (the lettering on the accent), `lettering` is the light one (on dark floors, plates and frames); the banners of the lobby keep P's family.

| What | Look |
|---|---|
| floors (`floor`) | glossy dark panels (luminance 0.02-0.1) in two near tones on a 2 m grid, thin glowing lines of P between them (emission 0.9 over a faint halo) |
| walls (`wall`) | dark steel panels with thin vertical light seams every 4 m in P's dim tone (`wall_brand` in S); the lit sun rakes across them |
| slab sides, curbs (`base`) | dark with a glowing line every 1.5 m |

| movers (`mover`, `mover_top`) | dark steel with accent caution bands (0.25 m) on the sides, a finer glowing grid on top |
| hazards (`danger`) | the same red as everywhere, with emission 0.55: it glows on dark |
| obstacle boxes, trim | dark metal around the cast's pictures (lit prints: the picture glows 35 % with its own colours), accent trim with emission 0.85; a near-black plate colour in `obstacles[].color` is lifted to a mid grey so it never vanishes (`TeamCast.obstacle`) |
| boards (arches, banners and billboards without a picture) | dark metal plates with white lettering, the picture or logo on them |
| lobby | the same floor, signboards on dark metal with the circle's banner colour on top, flags as light (unlit), the wardrobe rug dark with an accent band |
| hexagon tiles (level 15) | three dark tones one contrast step apart, a glowing rim in P; the warning and falling tones stay red |

| light | one warm low sun (1.4) and one flat cool ambient colour (the sky gives none); reflections from the sky; ACES at exposure 1.25; the environment's own glow (HDR threshold 1.0: only lines, trim, flags and the sun bloom) |
| world | a black void 60 m under the lowest walkable surface with a faint grid in P that fades with distance; 12 monoliths with light strips on the horizon (250-430 m, a few with a beacon); 22 floating hexagon platforms with a glowing rim on hexagon cones, wearing a monolith, a pylon with a beacon or a stack of three plates with glowing seams; 5 big light rings standing in the sky; the course on dark supports |
| bunting, confetti | light blades (thin, bright at the top, fading to the dark, swaying) where the party world hangs pennants (`bunting_rule.gd`, same places, no longer than a pennant); embers (small bright flat pieces) climbing and turning |

**Rules**: the party world's (`Boxes`, `Cylinders`, `Cones`, `Edges`, `Strips`, `Orbs`, `Rings` entirely 25 m under the course or 14 m from its box on x/z; `Supports` under the course's own ground; `Bunting` on the tall walls;
`Embers` columns never start inside the course's box below 8 m over its highest point; nothing has collision, casts a shadow or is a light), and for the palette (`tools/party_look_check.gd`, 69 palettes
in night): floors and walls dark but not black, the glowing lines at least 3.0 and the lettering 6.5 against every floor tone, the hazard red 1.8, hop blocks 1.6, the lines on the walls 2.0, the lettering on the plates 8, on the frames 4.5, tiles one step apart.

**Cost.** Phones stay smooth: one light (the sun) and one ambient colour, no extra light, no reflection probe, no texture; the sky is a few dozen ALU operations per pixel, stars and the sun only in the main pass.
At most 11 draw calls (the void plane, seven batches, supports, light blades, embers) and 500-1000 instances (95 % of them 3-12 triangles; about 10 000 triangles, a third of the party world's) at quality High, 55 % at Low (Android's default).
Emission carries all the light: the environment's glow is the same as the party look's (hdr threshold 1, additive, intensity 0.3), so night adds no glow pass. Measured with `tools/party_perf.gd` (offscreen, 1280x720, this machine): level 14 and the lobby draw 126 and 96 objects at about 1.0 ms
on the Mobile renderer (the party look 123 and 81 at 0.9 ms) and 3.3 ms on the Compatibility renderer (party 3.1 ms), with a fifth fewer triangles than the party look. On the Compatibility renderer there is no bloom halo, and the lines, trim and sun stay readable
as plain emission, brighter than their surroundings (`--rendering-method gl_compatibility` frames). Built in 2-10 ms the first time a level is shown, seeded like the party world.


## The stage look (the falling-tile rounds, levels 25-28: a level file with a `stage` section)

**World** (`stage.enclosure` `"b"` only picks another layout, seed + 1, of the same slime-lake world): the candy world (`style/candy_world.gd`, "CandyWorld" below: meadow, mountains, clouds, cloud wall, sea) centred on the arena with a round lake under and round the layers, plus an inflatable orange rail ring. Sizes are given in E, the big square tile's edge (3.5 m; a square layer is 22 E = 77 m wide, a hexagon layer about 110 m flat to flat); the level's origin is the middle of the first full layer's top face (the start layer's groups hang one gap over it) and +Z is the way the start view looks (the pads lie there):

The sky is not fogged. The colours are given as SCREEN colours in `style/candy_look.gd` and turned into scene-linear light with the inverse of the grade (`CandyLook.scene_linear`) so the grade brings them back (on Compatibility a sky's colour is not sRGB-encoded: `COMPAT_SKY_GAIN`). |

**Light** (`style/candy_light.gd`, numbers in `style/candy_look.gd`; every daylight scene uses it: the stage rounds and daylight races through `levels/level_environment.gd` `set_stage`, the lobby yard (no level), the podium room, the title stage): ONE sun `#FFE9C4` x1.05 (raised from 0.9 so lit tops clip) at 61 degrees up (azimuth 300 by default: the light comes from the far left of a +Z camera so shadows fall towards the camera and to the right, `CandyLight.apply_sun(sun, azimuth)`), soft shadows (blur 0.8, bias 0.05, normal bias 0.8, 60 m, two cascades; `Style.apply_quality` still cuts them on Low and may raise them on High), ONE cool fill `#D9F9FF` x0.6 at 49 degrees up, azimuth 74, no shadow (`CandyLight.make_fill`; folded into the ambient on a phone: world scale below 1, ambient set-up B), ONE flat violet ambient colour (`CandyLook.ambient_flat`: the cosine mean of a violet and blue gradient for an up-facing surface, linear (0.29, 0.05, 0.39); a flat colour leaves the sky's radiance map free for bright reflections and is exact on Compatibility), LINEAR tone mapping at exposure 1/1.3 plus the 33-point grade as `adjustment_color_correction` (`CandyLook.GRADE`: toe crushed, scene-linear 1.3 is white, 0.18 -> 0.394 sRGB, 0.5 -> 0.697, 1.0 -> 0.975), no fog in the Environment (the exp2 fog is inside the backdrop shaders, `style/candy_fog.gdshaderinc`), no glow, no SSAO, no SSR. Compatibility: without sun shadows (Quality Low) it is exact; with them the engine adds a shadow-casting sun to ambient and fill non-linearly, so `CandyLook.COMPAT_SHADOWED_A/B` scale sun, fill and ambient (fitted to Mobile). `tools/candy_light_check.gd` measures all of it on both renderers (white plane = sun colour x energy, grey ramp vs the grade, lit and shadow anchors of a white, blue and orange floor, sky colours); Mobile: white tile lit #FEFBFE / shadow #C5ADEC (target #FCF9FE / #C0ABF7), blue floor #0094F5 / #005AC7 (#005CBA), orange floor #FBAF52 / #AD6D3F (#BB672F), shadow / lit luminance 0.43 (0.41).

**Gloss and highlights** (`style/candy_surface.gdshader`, `style/candy_prop.gdshader`, `style/candy_look.gd` grade, `style/candy_sky_facets.gdshader`): glossy plastic and vinyl show a sheen that does not depend on the sun being in the right place. Both shaders add, as emission, an analytic sky probe: `glare_color` (a saturated pale cyan, linear 0.12 0.52 0.70) times `(1 - N.V)^3` (the fresnel: a floor brightens to light cyan towards its far edge, a ball or beam towards its silhouette) times a glossiness that is 1 at roughness 0.1, about half at 0.4 and nearly 0 at 0.55 (tiles) and 1.0 (felt), plus a soft broad glare patch (`patch_color`, pow 14 round the sun's reflection; it ignores shadows) and a small crisp glint where the view's reflection meets the sun (`sun_dir`, the default sun's direction: azimuth 300, 61 degrees; a surface shows no glint under another azimuth). No broad sun lobe (it would ignore shadows and wash the violet shadows out). No texture, no probe: the same on Mobile and Compatibility; `glare` 0 switches it off per material (`CandyMaterial.make({... , "glare": 0.0})`). The sky radiance (reflections of the engine's specular) is the gradient x0.35. The grade (`CandyLook.GRADE`) is a base curve to the power 1.06 with the shoulder cut: from display 0.906 up (scene linear about 0.95; samples 0.860 0.915 0.945 0.965 0.985 1.0 at inputs 0.78-0.94) everything is 1.0, and on Mobile `adjustment_saturation` 1.16 before the grade keeps meadow, slime and mountains from washing to pastel white (Compatibility 1.0), so lit tops, the lime meadow and the pink slime clip (V >= 0.98 on 40 % of a full frame). The sky facets are TONAL: 60 x 30 cells (about 6 degrees a triangle), four tone levels per triangle (fill 0.03 + 0.37 m^2, m = 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1), only a hairline edge (gain 0.05), weighted by cos^9 round the camera axis: strongest round the middle of the view, faint at the edges. A floor block (`block_kit` vinyl material) has `crease` 1: the pinched seam between two mats goes dark navy (a few dark accents, V < 0.2). The default course floor is `#0AA4FF` (`candy_course.gd`), floors are roughness 0.3-0.35.

**Cost.** `CandyWorld` 9 draw calls and about 115 000 triangles (`tools/stage_frames.gd --stats` prints `candy_world {draws, triangles}`); built in 65-70 ms the first time a level is shown (terrain, mountains and puffs are cached per layout, three layouts, and per lake size), then a few ms. A whole frame 40-57 draw calls and 300 000-350 000 triangles for the square round (almost all of it the tiles, shadow passes included).

**Tools.** `tools/stage_frames.gd` (offscreen binary only, see its header) renders named framings of a stage level with the real character rig and tile steps, all derived from the arena's own size: `start`, `phone` (844 x 390), `pads`, `trail`, `rim`, `layers`, `deep` (hex only), `ground`, `over`, `rear`, `down` (the light calibration), `below`; `--orbit=<n> [--arc=<degrees>]` for a camera orbit (flicker check), `--hook` to check the light and world switch between a show level and a stage level, `--stats` for draw calls and triangles. `tools/stage_frames.gd --strip=<n>`: n frames at 30 fps of the start courier's own tile going white, sinking and going 15 frames later; `--startz=<m>` moves the start framings for a start pad at that z. `tools/stage_clip.gd`: a drawn bot round with the real stage camera following one runner, frames at 30 fps. `tools/candy_world_frames.gd` renders the world alone. The frame tools' cameras use far 4000 like the game camera (`Camera3D` default): a shorter far plane cuts the cloud wall (2600 m) and the far mountains. `tools/menu_stage_frames.gd` renders the title and results stage.

## CandyWorld (`style/candy_world.gd`, `candy_mesh.gd`, `candy_backdrop.gdshader`, `candy_terrain.gdshader`, `candy_sea.gdshader`)
